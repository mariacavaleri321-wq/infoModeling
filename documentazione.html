Documentation

This section documents the design and development process behind Rome in Two Takes. It presents the different stages that led from the initial idea to the final website, including research, narrative design, content modelling, visual design and technical implementation.

Each stage reflects a series of deliberate choices concerning the structure, content and visual identity of the project.

Our Workflow

01 Brainstorming 02 Concept 03 Research 04 Prototyping 05 Content Design 06 Development 07 Integration 08 Testing
01

Brainstorming

The project began with a collective brainstorming phase in which different cities, films, themes and possible locations were considered. The aim was to identify a subject that could support both a meaningful visit itinerary and multiple interpretations of the same urban space.

During this phase, different possibilities were compared according to the availability of real film locations, their geographical distribution and their potential for creating coherent narratives.

02

Concept & Film Selection

After comparing the different proposals and watching and discussing the selected films, Rome was chosen as the setting of the project. Its strong relationship with Italian cinema and the persistence of recognisable film locations made it particularly suitable for the creation of a Live Museum of Movie Locations.

The final corpus was selected not only according to personal interest, but also according to the possibility of connecting different films through two complementary narratives.

The first follows Rome through different historical periods, observing the transformations of the city and of Italian society. The second focuses on women and urban space, exploring how female characters progressively inhabit, cross and negotiate different spaces of Rome.

03

Research

The research phase developed along different directions. Existing websites and digital projects related to cinema and film locations were analysed in order to investigate possible models of visualisation and interaction.

At the same time, research was carried out on the selected films, their historical and cultural context, the individual shooting locations and their relationship with the city of Rome.

The geographical distribution of the locations was also studied in order to build plausible itineraries that could be visited within a single day.

04

Paper Prototyping & Information Architecture

Before starting the implementation, the structure of the website was explored through paper sketches and preliminary wireframes. This made it possible to define the main sections of the website, the navigation system and the relationship between maps, narratives, film locations and documentation.

Particular attention was given to the distinction between the two narratives and to the possibility of accessing the same locations from different perspectives.

05

Content & Metadata Design

A common content model was defined in order to represent every location consistently throughout the website. Each location is connected to information about the film, the shooting location, the scene represented and its role within one or both narratives.

Different levels of description were also designed to allow users to access shorter or more detailed information according to their interests and level of knowledge.

Metadata Model

A shared metadata model was developed to ensure that information about films and locations could be represented systematically and uniformly.

Displayed Information Metadata Property Description
Film Title To be defined Title of the film connected to the location.
Director To be defined Director of the film.
Year To be defined Year of release.
Location To be defined Real shooting location in Rome.
06

Development

Once the general structure had been established, the website was implemented using HTML, CSS and JavaScript.

Development included the creation of the main pages, the implementation of the interactive map, the narrative navigation system and the mechanisms used to switch between different typographic themes and types of content.

07

Integration & Visual Design

During the integration phase, the different components of the project were brought together into a single coherent interface. Particular attention was given to maintaining consistency between the map, the two narratives, the individual location pages and the global navigation system.

A shared visual language inspired by cinema was developed to make the main functions of the website immediately recognisable.

Graphic Themes

Six typographic and graphic themes were designed according to different historical periods. Their design was based on research into the visual conventions, typography and layouts associated with each period.

08

Testing & Refinement

In the final phase, the different components of the website were tested together in order to identify inconsistencies in navigation, layout, content presentation and interaction.

The project was then progressively refined to ensure that maps, narratives, texts, metadata and graphic themes worked as parts of the same system.
